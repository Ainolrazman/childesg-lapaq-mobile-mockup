Data Insights Summary - (up to today) (N=220)

The cohort demonstrates strong protein intake (111.6% RNI) but faces severe micronutrient gaps in calcium (15.9%), potassium (7.9%), fiber (15.5%), energy (30.5%) and carbohydrates (27.9%). Vitamin A (68.6%), Vitamin C (40.3%), and Iron (70.3%) show partial compliance with wide variance. Calcium and Potassium represent the highest clinical priority — both showing 100% cohort non-compliance and directly correlating with the stunting rates identified in the state heatmap above.

The Double Burden Paradox

The data reveals a critical paradox: energy at 30.5% RNI coexists with Selangor's 21% childhood obesity rate. This pattern — called hidden hunger or the double burden of malnutrition — emerges when children receive micronutrient-poor, high-refined-carbohydrate meals. The body is simultaneously undernourished (calcium, potassium, fiber, iron) and metabolically stressed (Na:K ratio at 5:1, four times worse than WHO's 1:1 target). If this diet continues, the cohort will produce adults who are shorter, weaker-boned, more chronically infected, lower-earning, and 25–40% more likely to develop hypertension, T2D, or osteoporosis — all of which are preventable within the 12–24 month window.
